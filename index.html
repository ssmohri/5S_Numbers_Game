What is 5S?

5S is a Lean workplace-organisation method. The aim is to make work easier, safer, more visible and less wasteful by creating an organised and repeatable environment.

5S Step 1

Sort

Remove what is not needed

In Round 1, you needed only 1–49, but the workplace also contained numbers 50–90.

Those extra numbers represent unnecessary tools, stock, files, equipment or materials that make the real work harder.

Round 2: We will remove 50–90. The task and the 20-second time limit stay exactly the same.

Lean connection

  • Less searching
  • Less visual clutter
  • Less unnecessary inventory
  • Same worker, better system
5S Step 2

Set in Order

A place for everything

Removing unnecessary items helps, but you still have to search for the numbers you need.

In Round 3, the workplace will be divided into nine clearly defined zones. Each zone contains a known range of numbers, so you know where to look next.

Example: Zone 1 contains the earliest numbers, then the sequence moves through the remaining zones.

Real workplace examples

  • Warehouse location codes
  • Labelled tool positions
  • Clearly defined storage zones
  • Frequently used items placed close to the work
5S Step 4

Standardise

Make the best method obvious and repeatable

Now we go beyond “roughly knowing where things are”. The numbers will be placed in one consistent standard layout.

Everyone can understand the same system immediately, regardless of who is doing the work.

Round 4: Click 1–49 again. Same task. Same 20 seconds. Different process design.

Why standardise?

  • Consistent performance
  • Easier training
  • Problems become visible
  • A baseline for further improvement

What about Shine and Sustain?

ShineClean and inspect the workplace regularly. Cleaning is not only cosmetic—it can reveal leaks, damage, defects, unsafe conditions and deterioration.
SustainMaintain the standard over time. Without routines, ownership and discipline, a well-organised workplace can quickly return to its previous condition.
Important: 5S is not a one-off clean-up exercise. It is a system for making good workplace practices visible, repeatable and sustainable.

Conclusion

What did the game demonstrate?

You did not suddenly become more intelligent or more hardworking between Round 1 and Round 4.

The process changed. Removing unnecessary items, organising what remained, and creating a standard method made the same task easier and more reliable.

The missing-number challenge adds another Lean lesson: standardisation makes abnormalities visible. When the numbers were scattered, missing items were difficult to detect. In a standard pattern, the same gaps became much easier to see.

That is a central Lean idea: performance problems are not always people problems. Often, the system makes good performance unnecessarily difficult.

SortRemove what is not needed.
Set in OrderMake what is needed easy to find and use.
StandardiseMake the best method clear, repeatable and visually manageable.
Lean connection: 5S can reduce searching, waiting, unnecessary motion, excess inventory and errors—while also making abnormalities easier to see.
